INTRODUCTION TO PHISHING

Meaning of Phishing

Phishing is a type of cyberattack in which criminals pretend to be trustworthy individuals or organizations to trick users into providing confidential information. Attackers may use emails, text messages, phone calls, or social media messages to deceive their victims. The main aim is often to steal passwords, banking details or personal information (FTC, n.d.).

How Phishing Works

Phishing attacks usually follow several stages:

1. The attacker prepares a fraudulent message.

2. The message is sent to the intended victim.

3. The victim is encouraged to click a link, open an attachment or provide information.

4. The attacker collects the information or attempts to install harmful software.

5. The stolen information may be used for fraud, identity theft, or unauthorized access.

Types of Phishing

Email Phishing

This involves sending fraudulent emails that appear to come from trusted organizations or individuals.

SMS Phishing (Smishing)

This involves using fraudulent text messages to trick victims into clicking malicious links or sharing personal information.

Spear Phishing

This is a targeted form of phishing in which an attacker creates a message intended for a specific individual or organisation.

Voice Phishing (Vishing)

This involves using telephone calls or voice communication to deceive victims into revealing confidential information.

Effects of Phishing Attacks

Phishing attacks can result in:

- Financial losses.

- Identity theft.

- Unauthorised access to accounts.

- Exposure confidential information.

- Malware infections.

- Damage to an organisation's reputation.

The consequences may affect both individuals and organisations (FTC, n.d.).

Importance of Awareness

Users need to understand phishing techniques because attackers often use deception and urgency to influence their decisions.

Cybersecurity awareness helps individuals identify suspicious communication and take appropriate protective action (CISA, 2024a).

Conclusion

Phishing is a form of social engineering that exploits human trust and behavior. Understanding its different forms and possible effects is an important step towards improving personal and organizational cybersecurity.
