RECOGNISING PHISHING ATTACKS

Introduction

Recognising phishing attacks is an important part of protecting personal and organisational information. Cybercriminals often create messages that appear legitimate to convince users to perform unsafe actions.

Common Warning Signs

1. Suspicious Email Addresses

Phishing messages may come from email addresses that imitate legitimate organisations but contain unusual spelling or unfamiliar domains.

2. Urgent or Threatening Messages

Attackers may claim that an account will be suspended or blocked unless the user takes

immediate action.

3. Suspicious Links

Messages may contain links that direct users to fraudulent websites designed to steal login information.

4. Unexpected Attachments

Unknown attachments may contain harmful files or encourage users to install malicious software.

5. Requests for Confidential Information

Suspicious messages may request passwords, banking details or other sensitive information.

6. Unexpected Rewards

Some messages promise prizes, refunds or gifts to encourage users to click suspicious links. These warning signs are consistent with guidance provided by CISA (2024a) and the FTC (n.d.).

Practical Example

Consider the following fictional message:

"URGENT: Your bank account will be suspended today. Click the link below to verify your

account details immediately."

Warning signs include:

- The message creates unnecessary urgency.

- It requests sensitive banking information.

- It encourages the user to click an unverified link.

- The sender's identity has not been confirmed.

How to Verify Suspicious Messages

Users should:

1. Check the sender's actual email address.

2. Examine links carefully without opening them.

3. Contact the organisation through official communication channels.

4. Avoid responding to unexpected requests for confidential information.

5. Report suspicious communication.

Users should not rely only on logos or professional appearance because fraudulent messages can imitate legitimate organisations (FTC, n.d.).

Conclusion

Identifying warning signs allows users to recognize possible phishing attempts before sharing information or performing unsafe actions.
